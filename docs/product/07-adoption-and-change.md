# 7. Adoption and change

Status: v0.1, October 2026. Owner: product owner, with the head of procurement as sponsor.

A new intake process fails quietly: requesters keep emailing, officers keep answering, and the tool
becomes one more channel. Adoption is therefore planned as part of the product, with its own
measures and its own stop rule.

## 7.1 Who has to change what

| Group | What changes for them | What they gain | What they may resist | Response |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Requesting managers (occasional) | Answer questions instead of writing an email | A started TOR; one round instead of several; knowing what happens next | "One more system"; questions feel bureaucratic | Questions only for real gaps; channel moves to chat in the collaboration platform (US-14) |
| Procurement officers (daily) | Review a package instead of an inbox thread | Time back for judgment; split and framework checks done every time | Loss of control; distrust of AI recommendations | Every recommendation shows its rule; they approve, always; their disagreements improve the rules |
| Budget holders | Sign from the package | One signature, with the evidence | Little; the package is shorter than the thread | None needed beyond a one-page note |
| Head of procurement | Owns the policy file as a living artefact | Consistent application of the policy, audit trail | Fear of "AI approves purchases" | Human-control design signed before the pilot (06) |

## 7.2 Readiness checklist before the pilot

- [ ] Sponsor named and the pilot unit's director agrees to route all new service requests through the desk
- [ ] Policy file reviewed line by line by procurement; version recorded
- [ ] Request history for the pilot unit loaded and checked (week-1 data-quality measure)
- [ ] Three officers trained (45 minutes) on reading the package and recording agreement
- [ ] Human-control design signed (06); DPO note on the event schema
- [ ] Old intake email answered with an auto-reply pointing to the desk, for the pilot unit only
- [ ] Comparison unit receives the improved form and template only (the "stop" option as control)

## 7.3 Communication plan

| When | Audience | Message | Channel | From |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Week -2 | Pilot unit managers | Why: weeks lost before buying; what changes; what does not (people approve) | Unit meeting, 15 min | Sponsor + product owner |
| Week -1 | Procurement officers | How to read a package; how to record agreement or disagreement | Hands-on session | Product owner |
| Week 0 | Pilot unit | Start: one link, one-page guide | Email + intranet | Unit director |
| Weeks 1-6 | Requesters | What changed this week because of your feedback | Short note, fortnightly | Product owner |
| Week 7 | Leadership | Results against the kill criteria; go / iterate / stop | Two-page memo | Sponsor |

## 7.4 Champions and feedback

- One champion in the pilot unit (a frequent requester) and one in procurement (a senior officer):
  first users, first critics, present at every demo.
- Every package has a thumbs rating at confirmation (US-12); every officer return carries a note.
  Both are read weekly; confirmed problems become backlog items with a failing test before the fix.

## 7.5 Adoption measures and the stop rule

| Measure | Target by week 6 | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Share of the unit's new service requests entered through the desk | 80% | Case files vs procurement inbox |
| Requesters who use it again unprompted | 60% (H5) | Case files by pseudonymous id |
| Officers reviewing from the package alone | 3 of 3 | Week-3 and week-6 check-in |
| Thumbs-up share at confirmation | 70% | Usage events |

If under 50% of requests come through the desk by week 3, stop and interview: the problem is the
channel or the questions, not the users. Fix before continuing, or stop the pilot.
